# Story draft v2: "Aksara yang Terlupa", with Ciung Wanara embedded (for approval)

**Status: v2 APPROVED by the user, 2026-11-06.** Nothing in the game data has changed yet. Next: it replaces [story.md](story.md) Spine, Characters and Acts, and quests.md section 4 is rewritten to match (Tier O, plan mode first).

The user's decisions so far:
- The hero is the player's own character, not Ciung Wanara. The player can type a name in the New Game menu.
- Batara Kala stays.
- Keraton Galuh is a new region.
- Most important: the main story is an RPG story of its own, in the spirit of Skyrim, The Witcher or Assassin's Creed. Ciung Wanara is embedded inside it. It must not be a culture dump, and it must not be a Ciung Wanara quest.

So section 1 is the RPG story with no folklore names in it. Section 2 shows where the legend sits inside it.

## 1. The RPG story (the spine)

### 1.1 What we take from the reference games
| From | What we use |
|------|-------------|
| The Witcher 3 | A **personal hunt** drives the whole game: find one lost person. Recurring hunters chase you (the Wild Hunt). Contracts become spirit bounties |
| Skyrim | A **civil war in the background** that you get pulled into. Underneath it, a world-ending force that feeds on it (Alduin) |
| Assassin's Creed | A **hidden enemy behind the throne**, a court to get into, a mask to tear off, and history that turns out to be a lie |

### 1.2 Premise in one paragraph
On the night of the village wayang show, faceless raiders come out of the shadow screen. They take the hero's younger sister, **Laras**. By morning nobody remembers she ever lived, not even their own family. Only the hero remembers. In their hand is the strip of lontar where Laras practised writing her name, and its letters are fading. To bring her back, the hero must find out who the faceless are, who sends them, and where the forgotten go. The trail leads into a kingdom whose king is not who he seems, a war between two brothers, and finally the cave of the thing that eats what the world forgets.

### 1.3 The hero
- **Player-made:** the player types a name in the New Game menu, picks a gender for portraits and pronouns, and chooses a class (**ASSUMED**: name entry is new, see section 6). Dialogue avoids the name except on a few key cards, so any name works.
- **Role in the world:** the village dalang's apprentice. They carry the puppets, light the blencong lamp and know the stories by heart but can't write. That changes in the first hour.
- **Why the hero matters (the "Dragonborn" hook):** the hero can't be forgotten while Laras remembers them. She wrote the hero's name on the back of her lontar strip. Each holds the other's name. That's why the hero alone remembers her, and why the faceless can't wipe the hero.
- **Personal arc:** from someone who repeats other people's stories to someone who writes. At the end the hero writes Laras's name back into the world themselves.

### 1.4 The antagonists (layered, the Assassin's Creed way)
1. **The Faceless (Wong Tanpa Rupa, game invention).** People whose names were eaten: blank masks, hollow soldiers. They are the recurring enemy faction in every region, and they turn up wherever someone is about to remember something.
2. **The Nameless Hunter (Si Tanpa Ngaran, game invention).** The faceless's leader. He hunts the hero across the whole game, like the Wild Hunt. You meet him at the raid, again in Act I, II and III, and fight him for real in Act III.
3. **The false king.** The human villain: he sits on a throne with a borrowed face and pays for it with other people's memories.
4. **Batara Kala.** The force under everything. He eats the forgotten. The more people forget (and the more die in a war nobody will remember), the stronger he grows.

### 1.5 Structure and pacing (three acts, about 8-10 hours)
Every chapter has the same shape: a **goal**, 2-3 **leads**, a **twist** that changes the goal, a **boss**, and one **Laras beat** (a short dream or clue at a petilasan, 2-3 lines) so the personal stake never goes away.

### 1.6 Pacing rules
- **First 10 minutes:** the raid, the loss and the first fight. No lore before the hook.
- **A twist every chapter** that changes what you're trying to do, not just where you go.
- **The villain has a face early:** the Nameless Hunter appears at the raid. The false king appears in Ch 1, in a vision, or on a wanted poster with a face that keeps changing.
- **About 7 of 10 quest steps are play.** Talks are capped at 4 lines, and lore lives in the codex (the pacing rules from 2026-11-06 stay).
- **Laras beats:** every chapter, 1 short scene at a petilasan, never a long one.
- **Side content** follows the Witcher contract model: spirit bounties, the old legends as cursed "echoes" (optional bosses), and companion quests.

## 2. Where Ciung Wanara sits inside the story
The legend is the **political world** of Act I and II: the false king, the hidden heir, the tournament, the cage, the brothers' war, the river truce. The hero lives through it as an outsider with their own goal (Laras). The legend's beats happen in the legend's order, and the hero takes part in each one, but every beat is also a step on the hero's own hunt.

### 2.1 The legend (secondary sources agree)
1. Prabu Permana Di Kusumah, king of Galuh, gives his throne to his minister Aria Kebonan so he can become a hermit. The condition: do not touch the queens Dewi Naganingrum and Dewi Pangrenyep. Permana's magic makes the minister younger, and he rules as **Prabu Barma Wijaya**.
2. Barma Wijaya breaks the condition. The old king, disguised as the hermit Ajar Sukaresi, is killed and becomes the naga **Nagawiru**.
3. Naganingrum's newborn son is swapped for a puppy and set adrift on the Citanduy in a basket with an egg. Uwa Batara Lengser spares the queen and hides her. Pangrenyep's son is **Hariang Banga**.
4. Aki and Nini Balangantrang of Geger Sunten raise the foundling as **Ciung Wanara**.
5. Nagawiru hatches the egg into a fighting rooster. It beats the king's unbeaten rooster Si Jeling. Lengser recognises the prince, and the king grants half the kingdom.
6. Ciung Wanara locks Barma Wijaya and Pangrenyep in a strong prison (often told as an iron cage).
7. Hariang Banga fights him to free his mother. Neither wins. The parents stop them: fighting your brother is *pamali*. The land is divided at the river, now called the Pamali. Ciung Wanara takes the west (Sunda), and Hariang Banga the east (Java).

### 2.3 The game's additions (the codex says "legend" or "game")
- **The Faceless, the Nameless Hunter, Laras and the hero** are game inventions.
- **The pact:** to keep the borrowed face after he broke the condition, Barma Wijaya made a pact with Kala. He gave Kala his own true name. Kala lets him keep the face and the throne, and he feeds Kala what people forget, starting with the old king and the old script.
- **The Nameless Hunter is Aria Kebonan's true self:** the name he gave away, hollowed out, serving Kala. That's why caging the king doesn't stop the forgetting (Ch 2 twist). When you beat the Hunter in Ch 4, the man in the cage gets his own old face back. He stays caged, as in the legend.
- **Aksara reveal true faces.** Writing is a record of the truth, so drawn glyphs break glamour. That's why the script was made forgotten.
- **The other legends** (Sangkuriang, Bandung Bondowoso, Buto Ijo, the keris of Mpu Gandring) are "echoes" woken by the forgetting: cursed stories replaying. They are optional bosses, except the keris, which is the Ch 4 gate.

## 5. Sensitivity
- **Cockfighting:** it's in the legend, but today it is tied to illegal gambling and animal welfare (**VERIFY** the law before any codex line). The hero never bets or handles a rooster. The bouts happen off-screen or as a cartoon. The codex says the legend tells it, and the game doesn't endorse it.
- **Sunda and Java:** the legend's own message is brothers don't fight. Neither side is the bad side. Hariang Banga is never a villain.
- **Kala and ruwatan:** unchanged from story.md.
- **The Majapahit claim** (Hariang Banga as Jaka Susuruh, founder of Majapahit) is only ever "some tellings say".
